EVIDENCE QUOTES: copy the exact characters from raw_text, including its punctuation. Do not retype, re-punctuate, or tidy a quote. Keep quotes short (one sentence is usually enough) to reduce transcription risk. If a claim states a number, at least one evidence quote MUST contain that number verbatim; when you set contains_verifiable_numbers=true, make sure a quote with digits is present, otherwise set it to false.

SOURCING (strict): set sourcing=FIRSTHAND_OFFICIAL only when the fetched page itself is the official announcer (the company's or regulator's own site). OUT OF SCOPE — market-quote news: this digest tracks the AI supply chain, not the stock market. If the article's main subject is share-price or index movement (逆势反弹/涨停/大涨/大跌/板块异动/领涨/主力资金/成交额, or any "X股大涨" framing), it is NOT supply-chain news: set importance_score below 60 and importance_criterion=NONE, even when it names semiconductor or AI companies. The same applies to trading rules, ETF/杠杆/margin regulation, index reshuffles and buybacks, unless they directly change the physical chain (e.g. export controls restricting chip shipments, industrial policy funding capacity).

OUT OF SCOPE — broker research and banking process: sell-side research, ratings, price targets, position advice (调仓/加仓/减仓/配置建议/投资策略/金股), analyst 唱多唱空, and investment-banking process gossip (承销商名单/保荐机构/路演/询价/bookrunner) are opinions and deal mechanics, not supply-chain facts. Score them below 60 with importance_criterion=NONE, and never use their vocabulary in your own writing.

STRIP THE OPINION, KEEP THE FACT: when such an article also carries a real industrial fact (capacity, orders, price, technology, financing that funds capacity), discard the opinion framing entirely and headline only the fact. An article that is ONLY opinion, with no industrial fact underneath, scores below 60.

NEVER write a market-action headline. Your headline must state the industrial fact, never the price reaction. If a market-quote article also contains a genuine supply-chain fact, discard the market framing entirely and headline only the industrial fact — e.g. an article titled "A股半导体逆势反弹：长鑫科技将登陆科创板" must be headlined 长鑫科技启动科创板IPO (an SUPPLY_CHAIN_SHIFT/MAJOR_ORDER_CAPEX item about financing capacity), with no mention of the rally. IMPORTANCE SCORING — judge the observable industrial event, not the article's fame, tone, company size, or source language. The email UI deterministically converts importance_score to a five-star display (90-100 = 5 stars, 80-89 = 4 stars, 70-79 = 3 stars, 60-69 = 2 stars, 0-59 = 1 star); output the numeric score only and never write star symbols into any text field:
- 90-100: a rare, verified event with exceptional and broad supply-chain impact, such as a binding step-change in global capacity, a highly material enacted policy, a major delivered technical shift, or an unusually large order/capex commitment whose scale and transmission path are explicit in raw_text. Reserve this band for the day's genuinely highest-impact events; company fame alone never qualifies.
- 80-89: a verified event with broad or unusually large supply-chain impact, such as a major capacity change, binding order/capex commitment, material product or commodity price move, enacted policy change, delivered technical step-change, or major earnings/guidance revision.
- 70-79: a verified event that materially changes one important link, supplier/customer relationship, delivered capability, commercial availability, or demand/supply outlook, with a clear factual transmission path.
- 60-69: a new, concrete, verified event that directly changes the state of an AI supply-chain link and cleanly matches one named importance_criterion, even if it concerns one company, one region, or a moderate magnitude. Examples include actual commercial shipment or availability, disclosed capacity deployment, a signed order or long-term agreement, an implemented policy, a reported product contract/spot price change, a customer qualification, or earnings/guidance containing a new chain-relevant fact.
- 0-59: commentary, predictions without a new underlying event, routine marketing, minor feature updates, academic proposals, unexecuted aspirations, personnel gossip, stock-market framing, repeated statements, or facts with no direct supply-chain change.

Do NOT push a concrete qualifying event below 60 merely because it affects only one company, is not globally prominent, or is smaller than the day's largest story. Conversely, never raise a score to fill a quota: a day with few or zero qualifying items remains correct. The 60 threshold is a calibrated eligibility boundary, not a demand for sensational news.
